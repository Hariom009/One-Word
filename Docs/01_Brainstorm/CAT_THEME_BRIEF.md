# One Word — screen inventory for the Cat theme

Paste into Claude Design. Goal: a "Cat" appearance for both the Mac app and the iPhone app — palette, type, corners, animations, and which cat drawings sit where (and where none should).

## The app in one breath
- One Word: a word-a-day vocabulary app. SwiftUI, Mac + iPhone, bundled dictionaries, no network. Calm, editorial, word-first; chrome stays quiet.
- Every entry = headword, part of speech, Hindi gloss (Devanagari, optional), English definition, example sentence (optional). German dictionary headwords are German; Urdu headwords are Devanagari.
- 8 painted dictionary covers (Everyday English, Emotions, Philosophy, Corporate Slang, Idioms, Classical English, Urdu, German) are today the only colour in the app. Bookmarks is a 9th "book" without a cover.
- Free vs Premium (one-time purchase): Premium unlocks the 7 paid books, Practice, and cross-book search meanings. Locked things show a small lock.

## How a theme works today (what "Cat" has to fill)
- An Appearance picks a palette: background, surface, ink, muted, definition, example, accent, Hindi rule, hairline, a corner-radius multiplier (×2.2 turns controls into pills), an optional glow band across the top edge, and whether entry sections are frosted tiles or hairline-separated blocks. Existing: System, Light, Dark, Midnight (near-black, deep-blue band, glass tiles, geometric sans, pills), Umber (warm charcoal, parchment ink, brass rule, serif, flat). Cat must take a position the others don't.
- Two independent "Doodle" switches: hand-drawn icons (swap SF Symbols for drawings) and handwritten display face. Cat can supply its own icon set and/or face.
- 13 icon slots the icon set must cover: home, history, practice, bookmarks, dictionaries, search, settings, profile, feedback, learned (seal), selected (check), bookmarked (filled ribbon), premium (open lock). The filled bookmark ribbon is a drawn asset that keeps its own colour; the un-bookmarked state is the same drawing greyed.
- Display face: headword 64pt Mac / 46pt iPhone, italic part of speech 16pt, definition 22/19, example 20/17 italic, wordmark 18/20. Hindi 25/20 in the system Devanagari face. Two scripts share a column — line-height matters.

## Shell (Mac)
- Window has no toolbar. Each pane paints its own 52pt header strip: sidebar toggle, Back when pushed, "One Word" wordmark | pane title in muted text, then the pane's buttons as soft rounded tiles on the right.
- Sidebar (190–300pt): a "Search ⌘K" launcher pill on top; rows Home, History, Practice (lock when Free), Bookmarks; a "Dictionaries" section with one row; selected row's icon fills in. Bottom corner: account avatar + name (goes to Profile), a plan tag "Free"/"Premium" (goes to Premium), a speech-bubble feedback button. A one-time "Have a suggestion? One line is enough. Write a line ×" card sits above them on first launch.
- Painted appearances take over the sidebar ground so window reads as one surface; pane switches crossfade in 0.22s.

## Shell (iPhone)
- Five tabs: Home ("Today"), History, Practice or Bookmarks (Practice once Premium is owned, Bookmarks until then), Search, Profile. Dictionaries, Settings, Premium, Learned, Word Detail are pushed inside a tab. Header strip shows the pane title only (no wordmark), Back on pushed screens. Touch targets 40pt+.

## Screens
- **Sign In (the gate).** Whole window until signed in: wordmark "One Word", tagline "enrich your vocab by only one word a day", "Sign in with Apple" then "Sign in with Google" (same size), an error line if any. A 70%-dimmed busy overlay with a spinner covers the window during the browser round trip. Big open space above the wordmark — the natural home for a hero drawing.
- **Home / Today.** Mac header: today's date as title, a dictionary chip (short name → Dictionaries pane), "New Word" (shuffle: a peek that returns to today's word), Bookmark ribbon, Pronounce. iPhone header "Today", then a dateline row: weekday + date in small uppercase left, book chip + name + chevron right. Body is the word entry (below).
- **Word entry (shared layout, also Word Detail when pushed).** Headword + italic POS on one baseline; Hindi gloss indented behind a 2pt accent rule; definition; "USED AS" label + quoted example in a section (tile or under hairline), or "No example on file" with a closed-book symbol; "IN THE SAME VEIN · SYNONYMS" collapsible with related-word rows (term, POS, definition) that push another detail; a small spinner beside the label while related words build (~2s). Word change cross-dissolves. Locked word: headword only, then the Premium bar. Max column 720pt, 56pt margins Mac / 20pt gutter iPhone.
- **Bookmark toggle animation.** Ribbon stretches down 22% then settles; filled ribbon lands after. Pronounce speaks the headword (hi-IN voice for Devanagari).
- **History.** Mac: a 268pt left rail — weekday in caps, the day number large in the display face, month/year muted; a hand-drawn monochrome month grid with hover and "Go to Today"; a "Reading from" book row that opens a sheet "Read History In" (the bookshelf bound to History only, "Match app", Done, a note that Home and the widget keep theirs). Divider, then the word entry. Header: ← → day arrows, Bookmark, Pronounce. iPhone: the rail lies flat as a strip over the word, arrows flank the date, grid unfolds on demand.
- **Search.** Header search field "Search every dictionary" (autofocus, ⌘K). Rows: term in the display face (swells 1.85× under the Mac pointer), italic POS, Hindi gloss, a shelf mark at the end (book symbol + short name, lock if Premium). iPhone rows are two lines. Empty state: a thin hexagon mark, headline, the query in quotes, a footnote. Both Search and Bookmarks empty states are where an "nothing here" drawing could go.
- **Bookmarks.** Same list pinned to saved words; header shows ribbon + count (numeric transition) and "Search N words". Empty state uses the ribbon.
- **Dictionaries (the bookshelf).** A plank/board with a lit top edge; the chosen book stands face-out large on the left, the rest spine-out in catalogue order with gilt bands and the short title down the spine. Tapping a spine pulls it: it turns to face you as it grows into the front while the old one turns back and slides home (0.5s, with a page-pull sound). Caption under the front book: "N entries · Selected for word of the day" or "· Premium" + lock. Locked books stay put and shake three times with a buzz/haptic. Premium bar pinned at the bottom. Bookmarks is not on the shelf.
- **Practice (Premium only; Free lands on the Premium page).** One English sentence centred, rolls in like a slot reel (letters scramble, then land, with a sound); Space / tap reveals the German line beneath; again rolls the next. Hint text top-right on Mac, bottom on iPhone ("Tap to reveal the German, tap again for the next sentence"). Header: Pronounce. Optional auto-speak.
- **Profile.** Avatar 92pt (76 iPhone) + name; Edit → inline Cancel/Save capsules (in the header on iPhone), name field, a "Picture" grid of a dozen face tiles (boy, girl, dog, pumpkin, alien, redhead, businesswoman, …; account photo is one) — a cat avatar set belongs here; "Details": gender, email, member since. Two stat tiles: bookmarks (ribbon) and words learned (seal), both tappable. Optional "Toward German fluency" progress bar "N of target". Rows: Learned, Bookmarks and Practice (iPhone only), "Premium"/"Free plan", Settings. "Sign out" at the bottom.
- **Learned.** Pushed from Profile: a log of every word read, newest first, grouped by day; header Back + "Search N words"; hexagon empty state.
- **Settings.** Card sections with uppercase labels, rows split by hairlines, each switch explained by a line under it: Premium (the bar), Appearance (swatches System/Light/Dark/Midnight/Umber, with an "Aa" and an "ephemeral — lasting a very short time" preview; Cat goes here), Doodle (Hand-drawn icons, Handwritten type), The entry (Hindi meaning, Example sentence — applies to the widget), Practice (Practice mode, Speak the answer, Language: German), Progress (Fluency goal), Feedback (write in-app, or email).
- **Premium.** A spread, centred: the 7 paid covers fanned like a hand of cards across the top (100pt Mac / 72pt iPhone), title, then two tall cards — Free ("Everything a word a day needs, free always.") and Premium (Apple's localised price, "once", "Yours for good. No subscription, nothing renews.", Buy), "Restore Purchase". Once owned: six feature tiles (symbol in an accent disc + name + line: whole shelf, search every book, Practice, bookmark anything, German fluency goal, any book on your widget) and the shelf as a list with counts, "N words in all". iPhone stacks everything in one column.
- **Premium bar (inline card).** Covers + price + Buy + Restore, drawn like a Settings card. Appears under the shelf, first in Settings, and in place of a locked word's meaning.
- **Feedback sheet.** "Write to us" + "It reaches the developer with your name and email attached.", a text area, N/limit counter, Cancel / Send; then "Thanks — that's on its way." "I read every one." Done. Good spot for a small thank-you drawing.
- **Widget (Mac desktop / iPhone Home Screen; small, medium, large).** Pure white / pure black card following the system scheme only: headword (serif), POS in caps, Hindi, definition (medium+), example under a hairline (large), a circular-arrow refresh button top-right. It does not see the app's appearance today — a Cat widget needs its assets bundled into the widget target, so decide if it's in scope.

## Design asks
- Palette + face + corners + band/tiles for Cat, in a position Midnight and Umber don't take; light and dark both, or dark-only like them.
- The 13 icon slots as one cat set, and the bookmark ribbon's filled/grey pair.
- Where a cat drawing earns its place (sign-in hero, empty states, loading, avatar set, thank-you, hint, shelf ends, calendar's today) and where it must not (the word entry itself, the search rows, the covers, the widget unless bundled) — the word stays the hero.
- Any Cat-specific motion for the moments that already animate: pane crossfade, word cross-dissolve, bookmark stretch, book pull, locked-book shake, sentence reel, New Word shuffle, count ticks.
